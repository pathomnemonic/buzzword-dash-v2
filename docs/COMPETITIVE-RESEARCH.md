## 2. What reviewers say users want

Review sites are largely written by prep companies (Lecturio, Blueprint) or affiliate sites, so treat "consensus" claims with caution. Recurring themes in the sources I read:

- **Explanations that teach understanding**, not only test tricks: one review says UWorld is best at test-taking skill but not at teaching medicine. https://www.lecturio.com/blog/best-usmle-qbanks-2026-uworld-vs-amboss-vs-lecturio/
- **Integration between question bank, reading and spaced repetition** (AMBOSS' Anki add-on and article links are the praised part). https://www.amboss.com/us/pricing
- **Offline / low-connectivity use on rotations**: UWorld mobile needing a persistent connection is flagged as a weakness.
- **Cost**: price is a repeated theme and AMBOSS is described as cheaper than UWorld; students stack 3 to 5 subscriptions (Qbank + videos + Anki sync).
- **Anki is a review tool, heavy and long (one student: 6 hours a day for a year), not a learning tool**; users pair it with a Qbank. https://blog.blueprintprep.com/medical/quick-takes-on-usmle-step-1-resources/
- **Visual mnemonics are great for rote subjects but time-consuming** (Sketchy). https://testpreppal.com/usmle/study-tools/sketchy
- Burnout and gamification: **I found no usable review data**. Marked unverified; forum sentiment (r/medicalschoolanki) was not reachable.

Implication for Dx Dash: nobody in this set is a game, offline-first, free of ads, or cheap; its niche is the "5 minutes between things" retrieval-practice layer on top of a Qbank, not a Qbank replacement.

## 3. Positioning

- **One line:** "The fast, free-to-start board-review game that keeps your streak alive between Qbank blocks." Not "replace UWorld".
- **Against Anki:** same FSRS scheduling, but you do not build decks (3,000+ built-in clinical-vignette cards, Anki/AI import for your own). Anki's own App Store text complains about knock-off "Anki" apps, so do not use "Anki" in the app title; use it only as a keyword/compatibility line ("imports your Anki export").
- **Against UWorld/AMBOSS:** they are deep and expensive; Dx Dash is short sessions, offline solo play, multiplayer, a monster that punishes lapsed streaks. Claim only what `docs/STORE_LISTING.md` allows (no score guarantees).
- **Against Quizlet:** Quizlet is generic and increasingly paywalled; Dx Dash is board-specific (USMLE and COMLEX).
- **Audience tiers:** MS1-2 (Step 1 / COMLEX Level 1) first, then MS3-4 (Step 2 CK / Level 2), then PA/NP boards via the existing "pance" keyword.
- **Trademarks:** compare truthfully, never put a competitor's mark in ad headlines (see `docs/marketing/07-COMPLIANCE.md`).

## 4. Pricing observations

- The category's price ladder: free (Anki), about $36/yr (Quizlet Plus, unverified), $120/yr (Sketchy lowest annual plan shown, unverified label), $150/yr (AMBOSS yearly membership, Qbank extra), $350-650 (Qbank bundles, UWorld range unverified). Students pay for outcomes, in large lumps, timed to the exam date.
- **Time-boxed access is the norm** (UWorld 90/180-day, Sketchy 6/12/24 months, AMBOSS monthly Qbank packages). A "Board Season Pass" (3 or 6 months, one purchase) matches how users already budget, and avoids the subscription-fatigue complaint.
- **Free trials are 5 to 7 days** (AMBOSS 5, Sketchy 7), and Sketchy also offers Klarna instalments. Quizlet monthly in-app prices run higher than web prices (third party).
- A student-priced Dx Dash Pro near the $36-60/yr band (below Sketchy and AMBOSS, above a coffee) is defensible; one-time AnkiMobile at $24.99 shows students accept a flat price for a flashcard tool. Check `docs/MONETIZATION-STRATEGY.md` for the current plan before changing anything.
- Discounts: AnkiHub reportedly gives scholarships; AMBOSS lists group discounts. Cohort/group pricing (`docs/COHORTS.md`) fits this.
